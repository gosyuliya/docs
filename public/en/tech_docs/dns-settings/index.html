    How DNS Works with a VPN and Which Server to Choose

    DNS (Domain Name System) translates human-friendly website names (like example.com) into numerical IP addresses that devices use to locate each other online. Without DNS, you would have to memorize long strings of numbers instead of regular web addresses.

    By default, your Internet Service Provider (ISP) handles your DNS requests. This allows your ISP to see which websites you visit, even when your actual traffic is encrypted. Our VPN client prevents this and lets you control which servers process your requests.

    How DNS Works When Connected to a VPN#

    When you connect to a VPN, the app automatically routes all your DNS requests through an encrypted tunnel. This protects you from DNS leaks—a situation where your traffic goes through the VPN, but your domain lookups still go directly to your ISP.

    Here is what happens when you open a website:

    1. You type a website address into your browser.
    2. The request is encrypted and sent to the VPN server.
    3. The VPN server queries the selected DNS server to find the website’s IP address.
    4. You connect to the site, while your ISP only sees that you are connected to a VPN.

    Which DNS Server Should You Choose?#

    How to Change Your DNS in the App#

    If you decide to use a custom DNS server, follow these steps:

    1. Open the VPN app and go to Settings (the gear icon).
    2. Select the Connection or Network tab (the exact name depends on your operating system).
    3. Find Custom DNS and toggle it on.
    4. Enter the primary and secondary IPv4 addresses from the table above.
    5. Save your changes and reconnect to the VPN for the new settings to take effect.

    Frequently Asked Questions#

    Will a third-party DNS slow down my internet?#

    Usually, no. Major public DNS services like Cloudflare or Google are often faster than local ISP servers. However, if the DNS server you choose is located far away from your VPN server, your connection latency might increase slightly.

    What should I do if websites stop loading after changing my DNS?#

    Double-check the IP addresses you entered for typos. If the addresses are correct, the selected DNS server might be temporarily down or blocked on your current network. Switch the DNS setting back to Default and reconnect to the VPN.
